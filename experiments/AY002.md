# AY002 — Breath Response Characterisation Experiment

## Objective

To characterise the response of the BME688 gas sensor to controlled breath exposure and investigate how breath duration affects the magnitude of gas resistance change.

The aim was to quantify the percentage deviation from baseline and use this information to inform future breath detection algorithms.

---

## Hardware Setup

The experiment was performed using:

- ESP32 microcontroller
- BME688 gas sensor
- TFT display
- Serial Monitor for data logging

The sensor was calibrated under ambient conditions before each testing session.

---

## Experimental Conditions

During testing:

- Food and drink were consumed approximately 2 minutes before 5 second testing.

These conditions were recorded as recent food and drink intake may influence breath composition and sensor response.

---

# Part 1 — 3 Second Breath Exposure

## Method

1. Sensor baseline was established through calibration.
2. Approximately 3 seconds of breath exposure was introduced.
3. Maximum percentage change from baseline gas resistance was recorded.
4. The experiment was repeated across multiple trials.

---

## Results

| Trial | Breath Duration | Baseline Gas Resistance | Maximum Gas Resistance Change |
| 1 | ~3 seconds | 63.28 kΩ | 36.71% decrease |
| 2 | ~3 seconds | 63.28 kΩ | 37.01% decrease |
| 3 | ~3 seconds | ~65.98 kΩ | 38.73% decrease |

Average response:

**37.48% decrease**

Response range:

**36.71–38.73% decrease**

---

# Part 2 — 5 Second Breath Exposure

## Method

Following recalibration, the experiment was repeated using approximately 5 seconds of breath exposure.

---

## Results

| Trial | Breath Duration | Baseline Gas Resistance | Maximum Gas Resistance Change |
| 4 | ~5 seconds | 71.53 kΩ | 45.74% decrease |
| 5 | ~5 seconds | 71.53 kΩ | 46.80% decrease |
| 6 | ~5 seconds | 71.53 kΩ | 47.76% decrease |

Average response:

**46.77% decrease**

Response range:

**45.74–47.76% decrease**

---

# Observations

The BME688 demonstrated a measurable response to breath exposure.

Increasing breath exposure duration from approximately 3 seconds to 5 seconds resulted in a larger observed gas resistance response.

Comparison:

| Breath Duration | Average Response |
| ~3 seconds | 37.48% decrease |
| ~5 seconds | 46.77% decrease |

A baseline drift was observed during testing, highlighting the importance of baseline calibration and potential drift compensation in future detection algorithms.

---

# Next Steps
- Develop a breath detection algorithm using the experimentally measured sensor response.
- Test the algorithm across repeated breath exposures and normal ambient conditions.
- Optimise the detection threshold to minimise false detections while maintaining reliable breath recognition.
- Reintroduce automatic breath sample collection once detection performance has been validated.
